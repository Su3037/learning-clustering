Problem statement

Input

An undirected weighted graph; an initial bisection (fixed node count per side, for example AE | BCD).

Output

For each pass: the trial swap sequence (gains, cut before and after, locks), the cumulative gains and the best prefix; then the accepted bisection, the final cut and the stop reason.

Objective

Lower the cut by swapping pairs.

Constraints

Each swap trades one unlocked node from each side, so the side sizes never change; each node swaps at most once per pass.

Assumptions

Gains may be negative; ties go by the lexicographic order of (−gain, a, b); on a tie for the best prefix the shorter one wins; the empty prefix is allowed.

Stopping

A pass whose best prefix is empty (no positive cumulative gain), or the pass limit.

§ 1Improving a split with fixed sizes

The earlier methods all build a grouping from nothing. KL asks a different question: there is already a bisection; how do we make it better?

Picture two machine rooms: 2 cells on the left, 3 on the right, and the head count may not change. The more wires cross between the rooms, the higher the cost. The only move allowed is a one-for-one swap: one cell from the left trades places with one from the right. To compare fairly, this chapter always starts from a deliberately poor seed:

AE | BCD: the edges across are A–B 5, A–C 1, C–E 1, D–E 5, cut = 12.

initial bisection

二分种子

The bisection the improvement starts from. KL’s result depends on it.

swap

交换

One node from the left and one from the right trade groups. The side sizes stay the same.

KL was proposed by Kernighan and Lin in 1970 and is one of the classic heuristics for improving a bisection. The original author, FanqinZeng, introduced the fixed head count through a made-up story of five classmates being grouped; this chapter keeps that constraint and explains the rules on the course graph.

§ 2D values and the pair gain: why subtract 2w

external

外部边权

The total weight of v’s edges to nodes on the other side.

internal

内部边权

The total weight of v’s edges to nodes on its own side.

D(v) = external − internal

D 值

How much the cut drops if v alone moves across.

gain

收益

The cut before a swap minus the cut after it.

On the seed AE | BCD:

NodeSideExternalInternalD
Aleft5 + 1 = 60+6
Bright54+1
Cright1 + 1 = 24 + 2 = 6−4
Dright52+3
Eleft1 + 5 = 60+6

The gain of swapping a (left) with b (right):

g(a, b) = D(a) + D(b) − 2·w(a, b)

Why subtract 2w? Look at E and D: D(E) + D(D) = 9, but both D values count edge DE (5) as “external”. After the swap E is on the right and D on the left: they are still on opposite sides, and DE is still cut. The edge was counted twice too many, so take back 2 × 5: g(E, D) = 9 − 10 = −1.

The gains of the six candidate pairs: A↔D +9, E↔B +7, A↔C 0, E↔C 0, E↔D −1, A↔B −3. The largest is A↔D (there is no edge between A and D, so nothing comes off).

§ 3One pass: trial, lock, update

Within one pass, repeat:

  1. Among the unlocked left/right pairs, pick the one with the largest gain, even if the gain is negative;
  2. swap them (this is only a trial);
  3. lock both nodes: they don’t move again this pass;
  4. recompute D for the new grouping.

Pass 1 on G01:

StepTrial swapGainCut afterCumulative gain
1A↔D+93+9
2E↔C−811+1

At step 2 only E (left) and B, C (right) are unlocked: E↔C −8, E↔B −13, all negative. KL does not stop here; it takes the largest, E↔C, and the cut rises to 11 for now. No unlocked node is left on the left, so the pass’s trials end.

Why take negative gains

On this small graph step 2 can only make things worse; but on a bigger graph, a step that first makes things worse can “open up” a larger positive gain later. Only by finishing the whole pass can you see every prefix, and then decide how many steps to keep.

§ 4The best prefix, roll-back and stopping

prefix

前缀

The first k consecutive steps of a pass’s trial sequence, starting at step 1.

best prefix k*

最佳前缀

The prefix with the largest cumulative gain; k = 0 (do nothing) is a candidate too.

trial / accepted

试探 / 接受

The trial state is the temporary grouping mid-pass; the accepted state applies only the first k* steps to the pass’s start.

Pass 1’s cumulative gains: k = 0 → 0, k = 1 → +9, k = 2 → +1. The largest is k* = 1: from the pass start, apply only A↔D, accepting ABC | DE, cut 3. Step 2 is undone: it was only there to see whether anything better came later.

Pass 2 starts from ABC | DE with every node unlocked: trials E↔C −8, D↔B +2, cumulative −8, −6. Neither beats doing nothing: **the empty prefix k* = 0 wins**, the cut stays 3, and the algorithm stops.

§ 5The pseudocode, line by line

  1. repeat:
  2. start ← current bisection; trial ← start
  3. while both sides have unlocked nodes:
  4. compute D = external − internal for unlocked nodes
  5. (a, b) ← unlocked cross pair with the largest g = D(a)+D(b)−2w(a,b)
  6. swap a, b in trial; lock a, b
  7. k* ← prefix with the largest cumulative gain (k* = 0 allowed)
  8. current bisection ← start with only the first k* steps applied
  9. until k* = 0

Click any line for its explanation. When you step through the lab, the line being executed lights up.

How it maps to Python: lines 4–5 → best_swap(); line 7 → best_prefix(); lines 1–9 → kernighan_lin().

§ 6Limits and uses

  • A heuristic: reaching cut 3 on G01 does not mean KL finds the global optimum in general; it can stop at a local optimum.
  • Depends on the seed: different initial bisections can give different results; compare methods from the same seed. This module’s seed is the same as Module 08’s.
  • Keeps only the count: a one-for-one swap keeps the node count per side, not the area.
  • Cost: a naive implementation scans every unlocked cross pair at each step.
  • In engineering: KL and the next module’s FM are both used for local improvement after a spectral method or another initial partition. Changing one edge weight means recomputing every D value: for example, with A–B changed from 5 to 1 the initial cut becomes 8 and the gain of A↔D becomes 5.

07 / Code it · Python in your browser

Implement the largest-gain pair and the best prefix

Nothing to install: Python runs in your browser (Pyodide) and your code stays on this device. The checker is the same check.py as in the download.

Tasks (starter.py):

  1. best_swap(adj, side, locked): among the unlocked left/right pairs, find the one with the largest gain (the gain may be negative).
  2. best_prefix(gains): return (k, cumulative gain); k = 0 is valid.

Hint 1: first compute each node’s D = external − internal, then D(a) + D(b) − 2w(a,b) for each pair.

Hint 2: a prefix must be consecutive from step 1; update k only when the cumulative gain becomes strictly larger. The checker tests 120 random graphs: the side sizes never change, each node swaps at most once per pass, and gain = cut before − cut after.

08 / Common mistakes and limits

Where it goes wrong

Each entry gives the wrong result, the cause, a counterexample and the fix; every number in the counterexamples is recomputed by the tests.

Forgetting to subtract 2w

Wrong result
g(E, D) = D(E) + D(D) = 6 + 3 = 9, so E↔D looks like a bargain.
Why
Both D values count edge DE as external; after the swap E and D are still on opposite sides, so the edge is still cut: it was counted twice too many.
Counterexample
g(E, D) = 9 − 2×5 = −1; the real best pair is A↔D (no edge between A and D), g = 9.
Fix
g = D(a) + D(b) − 2w(a, b).

Stopping at the first negative gain

Wrong result
After A↔D in pass 1, every remaining gain is negative, so the pass is ended.
Why
KL finishes the whole pass before comparing all prefixes; on a bigger graph, a step that first makes things worse can be followed by a larger positive gain.
Counterexample
In pass 1 on G01, step 2, E↔C, has gain −8 and the cut rises to 11 for now: only a trial, undone in the end.
Fix
Keep trying until no unlocked cross pair is left, then choose the best prefix.

Treating any positive steps as a “prefix”

Wrong result
Picking all the positive-gain steps of a pass and applying them together.
Why
Later gains are computed with the earlier steps already made; skip one in the middle and the later gains no longer hold.
Counterexample
Pass 2’s trial gains are −8, +2: applying only the +2 step does not improve the start by 2 (alone it loses 12).
Fix
Take only a consecutive prefix starting at step 1; k* is where the cumulative gain peaks, and k* = 0 is valid.

Reporting the trial state as the result

Wrong result
Reporting the cut 11 at the end of pass 1.
Why
The state at the end of a pass has every trial applied; what is accepted is the state rolled back to the best prefix.
Counterexample
Pass 1’s trials end at cut 11; what is accepted is the first step: ABC | DE, cut 3.
Fix
Show trial and accepted separately; report only accepted.

Thinking count balance is area balance

Wrong result
With cells of different areas, still assuming a KL swap keeps both sides’ areas.
Why
KL’s one-for-one swap keeps only the node count per side.
Counterexample
Swapping a large cell for a small one keeps the count but changes the area.
Fix
When area matters, add a separate check or use a method with weighted balance; don’t reuse Module 04’s capacity field and pretend it is equivalent.

Scope and limits

  • KL is a heuristic: reaching cut 3 on G01 says something about this graph only; in general no global optimum is guaranteed.
  • The result depends on the initial bisection; compare methods from the same seed.
  • It keeps the node count per side, not the area.
  • A naive implementation scans every unlocked cross pair at each step, about O(n³) per pass; real implementations keep the D values sorted.
  • It only suits bisections with fixed side sizes; the next module’s FM relaxes that with single moves and a balance window.
