Learning Clustering / Eight modules / Clustering for EDA

Connect & Group

Blocks on a chip and circles in an address book are the same problem.

Thousands of cells on a chip are wired to each other. Put tightly connected cells together and the wires between groups become fewer, shorter and faster. Eight modules lead from “how do we score a pair of cells?” to “how should we group them so the critical path is fastest?”

01 / The route

Eight modules

The first three score and merge; label propagation and spectral methods are two ways to produce a first grouping; KL and FM improve a grouping; RW changes the goal to delay. Each module stands on its own.

Module 02

Affinity Metrics

Score every pair of cells: direct edge weight, shared neighbours, and pin-count on multi-pin nets. A score ranks pairs; it does not group them.

Capeur · film 3:21

 Module 03

Greedy Pair Merge

Start from single cells and repeatedly merge the pair with the largest inter-cluster weight. Value first, names break ties; stop at K or when nothing can merge.

thy031 · film 3:15

 Module 04

Size-Constrained Agglomerative Clustering

No cluster may exceed capacity C. Impossible, stuck on a wrong path, and legal-but-not-optimal are three different endings.

GateUltra · film 3:10

 Module 05

Label Propagation

Each node looks at its neighbours’ current labels and joins the group it is most strongly connected to. Names stay, labels change; it may settle, or it may swing back and forth.

Su3037 · film 6:20 + 2:34 + 4:54 + 2:10

 Module 06

Spectral Bisection

Build L = D − A, sort the nodes by the Fiedler vector, and try every cut inside the balance window. Flipping the sign only swaps left and right.

xingguangyaoyu · film 3:42

 Module 07

Kernighan–Lin Refinement

Swap pairs, lock them, keep exploring, then roll back to the best prefix. Trial moves may hurt; only the best stretch is kept.

FanqinZeng · film 3:18

 Module 08

Fiduccia–Mattheyses Refinement

Move one node at a time, keeping both sides inside the balance window. Only legal moves count; exploring, locking and the best prefix work as in KL.

stephQ-lab · film 3:45

 Module 09

Rajaraman–Wong Delay-Oriented Clustering

The goal becomes the longest path delay. Each crossing between clusters adds D, and one logic node may be copied into several clusters.

mapping pending · muranfei · film 2:60 + 2:22
